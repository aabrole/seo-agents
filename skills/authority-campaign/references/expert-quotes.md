# Expert Quotes via Journalist Request Platforms

Reporters and writers post requests for expert input. A short, specific, well-credentialed answer can earn a published quote with a link to the expert's site, usually on a topically relevant publication. It is among the most reliable low-cost authority tactics, but it needs a real expert and a daily habit.

## Platforms (verify before use)

The landscape changes often: platforms are acquired, renamed, repriced, or shut down. Check each platform's current status, pricing, and rules before recommending it.

- **HARO (Help a Reporter Out):** the original email-based service, changed owners and formats over the years.
- **Source of Sources:** free daily email digest of requests.
- **Qwoted:** platform matching experts to journalists, with free and paid tiers.
- **Connectively and Featured:** expert-answer platforms with paid plans and a publication-matching flow.
- **Help a B2B Writer:** free email list focused on B2B and marketing writers.
- Social search (journalists posting "looking for expert on..." on professional networks) works too, but follow the poster's instructions.

Start with free options. Pay for a tier only after a month of data shows the requests match the niche.

## Setup

1. Create each account with the expert's real name, real title, photo, and domain. Use a role email the client controls.
2. Set topic filters to the niche and adjacent topics the expert can speak about with firsthand experience.
3. Route all request emails into a dedicated folder or label so the inbox stays clean.
4. Keep a one-page expert profile: credentials, years of experience, notable work, and what they can speak to. Every fact is verifiable or `[CLIENT TO CONFIRM]`.
5. Add each account to the tracker's Credentials tab.

## Daily triage (15 to 30 minutes)

For each new request, decide in under a minute:

| Question | Answer needed to proceed |
|---|---|
| Is the topic inside the expert's real experience? | Yes, firsthand |
| Is the publication relevant and credible (real traffic, real editorial standards)? | Yes, check quickly |
| Does the outlet link to contributors or at least name them with the site? | Preferred |
| Can we answer within the deadline, with the expert's review? | Yes |
| Is the request asking for a specific profile (location, role, company size) we do not match? | If so, skip |

Skip anything off-topic. Several platforms ban or throttle accounts that answer outside their field, and generic pitching burns credibility with the writers.

Target volume: 1 to 2 quality answers per working day. A reply rate of around half is a healthy mix of published, pending, and unused.

## Answer structure

Draft the answer in this order, short paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences:

1. **Credentials and link up front.** One line: who the expert is, the most relevant proof, and the domain. Example: "I run a bookkeeping practice for independent dental offices and have handled about 300 month-end closes." The proof must be true.
2. **Direct answer.** Answer the exact question asked, first sentence. Follow the format requested (list, one paragraph, rating).
3. **Specific, non-obvious insight.** One detail only someone with experience would know: a number from their own work, a mistake they saw, a rule of thumb, a counterintuitive result. Use only real data and label estimates as estimates.
4. **Optional supporting link.** A link to a page on the client site that supports the answer (a guide, data page, case study). Include it only if it is genuinely useful and the platform allows links in answers.
5. **Signature line.** Name, keyword-relevant title, and domain, for example: "Maria Alvarez, Practice Owner and Dental Bookkeeping Specialist, example-brand.com". Keep the title truthful and tied to the niche; do not invent titles or inflate seniority.

Keep to the requested length. Short, scannable answers get used more.

## Substance rules

- The expert supplies the substance: the story, the number, the opinion. Collect it with 3 questions (what happened, what would you tell a beginner, what is commonly misunderstood), a short call, or a voice note.
- AI can help structure and tighten the expert's words. It cannot supply the experience, stats, or examples. Generic AI answers are obvious to journalists, are penalized by some platforms, and add nothing as a source.
- Do not tell reporters what they want to hear, exaggerate credentials, or fabricate figures.
- The expert reads and approves the final text before it is sent.
- If the request wants anonymity, follow the platform's process and never include the brand in a way that breaks it.
- Do not paste the same answer into multiple requests.

## Drafting workflow

1. Pull the day's candidate requests and mark go or skip with a one-line reason.
2. For each go: draft the answer shell in the tracker (status `drafted`) with the expert questions that must be answered.
3. Expert fills in the substance and approves.
4. Human sends or submits through the platform, then status becomes `submitted`.

## Follow up and track

- Record the request, outlet, date submitted, platform, and answer text in the tracker.
- Do not nag journalists. If the platform has a status view, check it; otherwise wait 2 to 4 weeks.
- When a quote appears, record the live URL, whether the link is present, follow or nofollow, and the anchor or mention text. Search the expert's name and brand weekly to catch unannounced publications.
- If the outlet published the quote without a link, a short, polite note asking for a link to the source is acceptable once; accept a no.
- Share published coverage on the brand's social profiles, add logos to a "As seen in" block only for outlets that actually published the quote, and add each item to the press kit coverage list (see `press-kit.md`).
- Review monthly: acceptance rate, outlets, relevance, and links earned. Drop platforms that send mostly irrelevant requests.

## Realistic expectations

- Many answers are never used. Treat 20 to 40 percent publication as good.
- Many links are nofollow or brand-only mentions. They still add independent sources about the brand and referral visits.
- A few good placements per month, compounded over a year, build a stronger profile than occasional big pushes.
