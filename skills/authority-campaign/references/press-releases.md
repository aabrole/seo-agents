# Press Releases

Press releases have two separate uses. Decide which one you are doing, because the structure, expectations, and success measure differ.

1. **Genuine news.** A launch, funding, partnership, award, study, or milestone that a journalist might cover.
2. **Brand-positioning release.** A well-sourced, factual release that explains what the brand is and why it can be trusted, distributed through low-cost newswires so that "what is [brand]" and "is [brand] legit" AI answers and search results have independent third-party pages to draw on.

Both are drafted for the human to approve. Buying distribution is the human's decision; this skill does not pay or submit.

## Realistic expectations

- Newswire links are usually nofollow, or on low-value syndication pages. Do not buy a release to move rankings.
- The value is brand-answer coverage: indexed, dated, third-party-hosted pages that restate verifiable facts. Some AI assistants and search results surface these when asked about a brand that has little other coverage.
- A genuine newsworthy item can also attract real journalist pickup, but that is the exception for small brands.
- Syndication quality differs by vendor and package. Check the sample distribution lists and recent live examples before choosing.
- Releases are optional. If budget is tight, spend the time on expert quotes and directories first.

## Use 1: Genuine news

Only issue a release when there is something factual and new. Examples: a product launch with real specs, a published study with data, a partnership, a notable customer count (with permission), an award, an event.

Structure:

- **Headline:** brand name, the news, and the niche context, in plain language. Under about 100 characters.
- **Subhead:** one supporting line with the key fact.
- **Dateline and lead:** city, date, and a first paragraph that answers who, what, when, where, and why it matters.
- **Body:** 3 to 5 short paragraphs: detail, one quote from a named spokesperson, context.
- **Quote:** from a real person who has approved it.
- **About [Brand]:** the boilerplate (same text used in the press kit).
- **Contact:** name, email, phone, and the link to the press kit page.
- Images with alt text and captions where the wire allows.

## Use 2: Brand-positioning release

The goal is a factual, source-backed explainer on a third-party domain.

Topics that work:

- "[Brand] announces [real, verifiable thing]" such as a new location, a certification, a policy, a published methodology, or a year in review with real numbers.
- A factual brand profile release: what the company does, who it serves, how it is priced, how it handles customer data or refunds, who runs it.
- A release that answers a reputation question directly with proof: licenses, registration numbers, public reviews counts (with sources), compliance statements.

Rules:

- Every factual claim has a source, or is verifiable on the client's site. Use `[NEEDS SOURCE]` for gaps and do not publish until they are closed.
- No invented awards, customer counts, rankings, or testimonials. No superlatives without evidence ("best", "leading") unless backed by a named, linked source.
- State the facts the questions ask about: who owns the company, where it is based, since when, what the product does, how pricing and cancellation work, how to contact support.
- Avoid comparative claims about competitors unless they are factual, current, and sourced.
- Write it as a release, not a disguised ad. Clear, flat language.
- Include an explicit label such as "Press Release" or "Sponsored press release" as the wire requires, and keep the byline and contact real. A release is promotional material by the brand and should read as such; it should not pose as independent journalism.
- Link to the brand site, the press kit page, and a few primary sources.

## Choosing distribution

- Low-cost newswires (for example AB Newswire and similar budget services) are the usual choice for brand-positioning releases. Premium wires cost more and reach more outlets; the extra reach rarely matters for brand-answer coverage.
- Free release sites exist, but many are low quality or deindexed. Test whether past releases are indexed before using one.
- Check current pricing and package contents on the vendor's site on the day you recommend it. Prices, syndication networks, and link policies change. Do not quote remembered prices.

Budget tiers (rough guidance, not prices):

| Tier | Approach |
|---|---|
| No budget | Skip paid wires. Publish the same news on the brand's own newsroom page and the press kit, and rely on expert quotes and directories. |
| Low | One brand-positioning release on a budget wire, once or twice a year. |
| Medium | Budget wire for the brand release, plus a mid-tier wire for a genuinely newsworthy event. |
| High | Premium wire only for major news a journalist would cover, combined with direct pitches to relevant writers. |

Frequency: 1 to 2 per year is plenty for a small brand. More releases with thin news dilute credibility.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the purpose (news or brand-positioning) and the questions it should answer.
2. Gather facts and sources into the brand fact sheet. Mark gaps.
3. Draft the release and a one-paragraph summary for the vendor form.
4. Expert or founder approves the quote and claims.
5. Run the draft through a claim check: each sentence with a number, name, or superlative gets a source note.
6. The human reviews the vendor options, pays, and submits.
7. Log in the tracker: vendor, date, cost, URL of every live syndication, follow status.
8. Add the release to the press kit coverage list and link it from the brand's own newsroom page.

## Measure

- Re-run the Step 1 AI answer questions (see SKILL.md) 2 and 6 weeks after publication.
- Note whether answers mention the release, cite the wire domain, or become more accurate on the facts you stated.
- Check whether the release is indexed (search the exact headline in quotes).
- Track referral clicks and new referring domains for completeness, but judge success mainly on brand-answer coverage.
- If nothing changes after 8 weeks, do not buy a second release. Strengthen the press kit page and directory profiles instead.
