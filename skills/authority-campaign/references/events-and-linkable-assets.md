# Events and Linkable Assets

Two slower tactics that produce more durable links when they work. Both are less certain than directories and expert quotes, so run them after the basics are in place.

# Part 1: Event and Meetup Link Building

Events produce links from event pages, organizer sites, sponsor lists, and recap posts, and they build real relationships in the niche.

## Ways to earn links from events

1. **Co-host or sponsor events run by others.** Offer a speaker, a space, a prize, or promotion to the organizer's list. In return, ask for an event listing and a brand description with a link on the event page and in announcement emails.
2. **Host your own events.** Webinars, meetups, workshops. Create a landing page on your domain for each event and list it on event platforms that link back.
3. **Event listing pages.** Submit events to community calendars, niche event sites, university and association calendars, and event platforms. Choose the ones that link to a landing page.
4. **Speak at events.** Speaker pages on conference sites often include a bio and link.
5. **Recap content.** Publish a recap with photos, a slide deck, and a thank-you to the organizers; they usually link or share it.

## Meetup groups

Community groups on meetup platforms can be a long-term asset.

- **Start or revive.** Search for inactive groups in the niche and city (no events for a long time, organizer gone). The platform may offer a path to request organizer status, or you can start a new group. Follow the platform's rules and terms and never impersonate previous organizers or mislead members.
- **Taking over a group:** only through the platform's official process, and only if you intend to run it. Be transparent with members about the change and the new focus, keep the group's original purpose where relevant, and hold real events. A group that exists only to hold links gets reported and removed.
- **Name and description:** describe the group honestly using the niche and city (for example "[City] [Niche] Meetup"). The description can mention the brand as the organizer and link to a relevant page. Disclose affiliation clearly.
- **Content:** monthly events (in person or online), consistent topics, and recorded sessions.
- **Costs:** platforms charge organizer fees; check current plans. Pay only if you will run events.
- **Best for:** large cities and active niches, and where in-person attendance is plausible. Online events work for remote niches.

## Co-hosting playbook

1. Search the niche's event calendars and event platforms for organizers in your city or topic.
2. Shortlist 10 to 20 events or organizers by relevance and audience size.
3. Draft an outreach message for the human to send:

> Hi [Name], I run [group/brand] in [city]. We'd like to co-host or support your [event]. We can offer [speaker, venue, promotion to our N members]. In return we'd like a short description of our group with a link on the event page and in the announcement email. Open to a quick call?

4. Agree on the blurb. Write it as a plain statement with a deep link, for example: "Co-hosted by [Group], a [niche] community in [city] run by [Brand]. Learn more at [page URL]." Keep it factual and distinct from the group's own description.
5. Ask whether attendees can opt in to your updates. Do not ask for or use a list of attendee emails unless the organizer shares it with the attendees' consent and in line with privacy law and platform rules.
6. After the event: confirm the link is live, send a thank-you, post a recap, and log the result.

## Event tracking

Columns: event or group, organizer, date, role (co-host, sponsor, speaker, listing), link target, live URL, link type, attendees, and follow-up status. Record leads and relationships, not only links.

## Expectations

- Slow and experimental. Expect 2 to 3 months before the first links.
- Cost is mostly time. A virtual assistant can handle research and outreach drafts, but the human sends and runs events.
- Side benefits: credibility ("organizer of the local [niche] community"), warm intros for outreach, and content.

# Part 2: Linkable Assets

A linkable asset is something on your site that people link to because it is useful, interesting, or new. It is the highest-effort and most uncertain tactic here, so choose the asset type where the brand has a real advantage.

## Asset types

1. **Free tools and micro-SaaS.** A calculator, checker, generator, converter, or template tool that does one thing well. Build it on your domain or a subdomain, add share and embed options, and target a buying-adjacent keyword (for example "free [thing] checker").
2. **Original data.** A survey, an analysis of your own anonymized data, or a benchmark report. Publish methodology and sample size. Fabricated or exaggerated numbers destroy credibility.
3. **Templates and swipe files.** Spreadsheets, checklists, document templates, and playbooks that practitioners copy.
4. **Reference pages.** Glossaries, statistics roundups, and comparison tables that are better than anything on page one.
5. **Newsletters and podcasts as assets.** Give each a landing page on the main domain so links to it consolidate with the brand site. Add archives, an easy subscribe flow, and directory listings for both.
6. **Interactive visuals and maps.** Only if the data is real and the interaction adds meaning.
7. **Stunts and challenges.** Hard to predict and brand-risky. Do not do anything misleading, spammy, or defamatory.

## Choosing an asset

Score candidates (1 to 5 each): relevance to the buying audience, how many sites in the niche would cite it, effort to build, effort to maintain, and uniqueness versus existing assets. Pick the highest score and ship a small first version quickly.

Check the SERP for the target keyword: if the top results are weak, an asset can win both rankings and links. If they are strong, find an angle they lack.

## Building

- Tools: scope to one feature. Provide a clear result, no required signup for the core feature, a short explainer, and a prominent link to the relevant service or product page. Use `seo-schema` and `schema-markup` for structured data where appropriate.
- Data: publish tables, charts, and downloadable CSVs with a citation line that includes the brand name and URL.
- Templates: allow copying or download without a wall; capture emails only for optional extras.
- Always include the brand fact checks: sources, dates, and the author.
- Add share buttons and an embed snippet that includes an attribution link, using the correct rel attributes.

## Promotion

A good asset does not promote itself.

1. Launch on relevant platforms (see `directories-and-launches.md`) if it is a tool.
2. Send it to the people who already cite similar resources: resource pages and "best tools" lists in the niche. Use `linkbuilding` resource-page tactics and the backlink tool to find pages linking to competing assets.
3. Offer it to newsletter writers and journalists with a clear one-line value statement (draft for the human to send).
4. Share on communities where self-promotion is allowed, with disclosure.
5. Mention it in expert quotes and podcast show notes when relevant.
6. Update and re-promote when data or features change.

## Measure

Track referring domains to the asset page, rankings for its target keyword, usage, and conversions to the main offer. Review after 90 days. Retire or fold assets that earn nothing and cost maintenance.
