# Podcast Guesting

Appearing on relevant podcasts earns a link in the show notes, a page on the host's domain that names the brand and the guest, an audio and often video asset, and new audience. Hosts also introduce guests to other hosts, so the first few appearances make the rest easier.

For the tactic's place in the link strategy see `linkbuilding` (podcast playbook). This file covers the operating procedure.

## Matching platforms versus cold pitching

| Approach | When it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Guest-matching platforms (for example PodMatch, Matchmaker.fm) | Default for most guests | Hosts are actively looking for guests. Verify current pricing and free-tier limits before recommending; both change. |
| Cold pitching hosts directly | The guest is already known, has a strong story, or the show is highly targeted | Lower reply rate for unknown guests; keep it short and specific. |
| Word of mouth and introductions | After a few appearances | Ask each host for one introduction. |
| Show-notes directories and show databases | For research | Use for finding relevant shows, not for pitching in bulk. |

Start with a platform, add targeted cold pitches for shows that are an especially good fit, then lean on referrals.

## Profile setup

A complete profile does most of the work. Fill every field:

- Real name, photo, location, and a short intro video if the platform supports it.
- Bio in two lengths, credentials that are verifiable, and links to the site and public profiles.
- 4 to 6 speaking topics, each with a one-line description of the angle and a specific takeaway.
- Past appearances (real), sample clips, or a speaking reel if available.
- Tags for the niche and related topics.
- Availability and tech set-up (good microphone, camera optional).

Add the profile to the tracker's Credentials tab.

## Choosing shows

Score each candidate show from 0 to 2 on each item, and pursue those scoring 7 or more of 10:

1. **Topic fit:** the audience would plausibly buy from or refer the brand.
2. **Active:** published within the last 60 days, regular schedule.
3. **Audience size:** a modest audience is fine. Mid-sized shows are often easier to book and less saturated than the biggest ones.
4. **Show notes:** hosts publish a page per episode with links to guests, not just an audio feed. Check recent episodes.
5. **Quality:** clear audio, thoughtful questions, real guests.

Unrelated shows are acceptable when the guest would be a good guest and the show has strong show notes, but prioritize niche-relevant ones.

## Avoid pay-to-play

- Do not pay to be a guest. Hosts who charge guests for an "appearance" are selling placement, not interviews, and the audience is usually small or fake.
- Paid placements may be fine as sponsorship if clearly labeled as sponsored, but that is advertising, not guesting. Treat it as a separate paid-ads decision for the human.
- Red flags: guaranteed downloads, "interview package" fees, no listeners or reviews, a feed full of paid guests.
- Check the show's recent guests, social proof, and reviews before replying.

## The pitch (draft for the human to send)

Short and specific. Platform DM: 2 to 3 sentences. Email pitch: under 150 words.

Template:

> Hi [Host], I listen to [show] and enjoyed [specific episode and detail]. I [role] at [brand] and can talk about [topic 1] and [topic 2], including [specific takeaway your listeners could use]. Links: [profile], [clip or past episode]. Happy to share a one-page guest sheet if useful. Thanks, [name]

Rules:

- Reference something real about the show.
- State credentials once, factually.
- Offer two or three episode angles, not a generic bio.
- Follow up once after 7 to 10 days; stop after that.
- The human sends from the guest's own account.

## What to send hosts after booking

A guest sheet in a shared document or email:

- **Bio:** 2 to 3 sentences, third person, credentials verifiable, and the brand name. Include a keyword-relevant descriptor of what the brand does.
- **Show-notes description (exact text):** write the paragraph you would like the host to paste. 3 to 5 sentences with the guest name, title, brand, the topic of the conversation, and deep links to relevant pages (for example the service page or a relevant guide), not just the homepage. Use the most relevant page for each mention and include the press kit page link.
- **Links:** website, 2 or 3 deep links, and social profiles.
- **Headshot and logo:** high resolution, with permission to use.
- **Intro line:** how to pronounce the name, and the one-line intro.
- **Topics and talking points:** 3 to 5 for the host's reference.
- **A suggested call to action** to say on air (a page URL that is easy to remember).

Use descriptive text in the show notes, such as the brand name and what it does, rather than keyword-stuffed anchors. Supply images where the host's site accepts them, with alt text.

## Prep for recording

- Review the show's format and recent episodes.
- Prepare 3 stories or examples drawn from real client work, and verify any numbers before the call.
- Do not claim results the brand cannot back up.
- Test audio, light, and connection.

## After the episode

1. Ask the host for the live URL and check the link in the show notes (follow or nofollow, anchor, target page). Log in the tracker.
2. Share the episode on the brand's profiles, tag the host, and thank them.
3. Add the episode to the press kit coverage list.
4. Repurpose: ask the host for the raw video or audio file if allowed, then clip highlights. Hand clips and captions to `video-seo` for short-form and YouTube publishing, and use the transcript to draft a blog or social recap.
5. Post a monthly roundup of appearances on the site, which also gives each host a backlink.
6. Ask for one referral to a similar host.

## Cadence

- Weekly: 5 to 10 platform responses or pitches, 1 to 2 recording sessions, 1 to 2 follow-ups.
- Monthly: aim for 2 to 4 published episodes after a ramp-up of about 6 weeks.
- Quarterly: review which shows delivered links and referrals; prioritize similar shows.

## Tracking

Tracker columns apply: show, host, platform, status (pitched, booked, recorded, live), recording date, publish date, live URL, link type, follow status, anchor, target page, and the clips created.
