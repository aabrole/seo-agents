# Directories and Launch Platforms

The cheapest, most reliable part of the program. Directories give a baseline of independent mentions and links; launch platforms give a burst of attention and coverage. Both are prepared as drafts for the human to submit.

## Part 1: Directories

### Find relevant directories

Start with relevance, not volume. A niche directory with real visitors beats fifty generic ones.

1. **Search patterns** (swap in the niche): `[niche] directory`, `[niche] tools list`, `best [niche] resources`, `[niche] newsletter directory`, `[niche] software directory`, `submit your [niche] site`, `add your business [city]`.
2. **Competitor lift.** In the backlink tool, list referring domains of 2 or 3 competitors, filter to pages that look like listings or directories, and keep the ones that accept submissions.
3. **Category lists.** Software and app directories, agency and service directories, startup lists, AI tool directories (if relevant), industry association member pages, chamber of commerce or local business association pages, and review or comparison sites that take vendor listings.
4. **General business directories** are acceptable only if they are real sites with editorial review and traffic. Skip any directory whose page is stuffed with unrelated categories and ads.
5. **Quality screen** before adding a target:
   - Does it have organic traffic or an active audience? (Ahrefs overview or a quick site search.)
   - Is the listing page indexable (no noindex, not blocked)?
   - Does it link to the listed site, and is the link follow or nofollow? Either is acceptable; record it.
   - Are the other listings real businesses, not spam?
   - Is it free, or is a fee clearly stated? Pay only for directories with real audience and editorial review.

Aim for 30 to 60 well-matched targets for a first pass rather than hundreds.

### Local citations

For businesses with a physical location or service area:

- Hand NAP (name, address, phone) consistency and the aggregator and citation list to `seo-local`. This skill does not duplicate that work.
- Main data aggregators and major platforms feed many smaller sites, so correct data there first.
- Use the exact NAP format from the Google Business Profile in every listing. Variations (Suite vs Ste, Street vs St) create duplicates.
- For service-area businesses, hide the address where the platform allows it and follow its rules.

### Prepare the submission pack

Write once, reuse with small variations (avoid pasting the identical text everywhere):

- 3 descriptions: 25 words, 50 words, 150 words. Descriptive, specific, readable, and written for humans. Include what the product or service is, who it is for, and where, and use the niche keyword naturally once or twice.
- 1 tagline (under 60 characters) and 1 alternate.
- 5 to 8 category and topic tags.
- Logo, 2 to 3 screenshots, founder or team photo if the directory takes one.
- Contact email on the brand domain, phone, address (if applicable), social profile URLs.
- Founding year, pricing summary, and any verifiable awards or certifications (`[CLIENT TO CONFIRM]` if unknown).

Complete every field the directory offers. Incomplete listings rank lower within the directory and may sit in a "new" or unreviewed view.

### Which URL to submit

Where the directory allows a deep link, submit the most relevant page for that listing, not always the homepage:

- Service directory for a specific service: that service page or its hub page (see `bofu-hub-pages` if in use).
- Software directory: the product page or pricing page.
- Local listing: the location page.
- General directory with one URL field: homepage is fine.

Mix targets across pages over time so no single page receives all links. Make sure the target page is indexable, loads fast, and has a clear page for that audience before pointing links at it.

### Submitting and logging

- The human submits (many directories require email verification, captcha, or payment). Prepare the pack and a per-site checklist.
- Use the tracker: tactic = directory, target, URL, status, submitted date, live URL, follow status, anchor, and who owns the login.
- Record the exact text submitted so it can be updated later, and so inconsistencies are easy to spot.
- Batch: 10 to 20 submissions per week. Do not submit everything in one day.
- Re-check status after 2 to 3 weeks. Some directories approve slowly or never; mark dead after two follow-ups.

### Paid shortcuts

Services that submit to a list of directories for a fee exist. If the client wants one:

- Check the list of directories before paying and compare it to your own screen above.
- Avoid anything that promises hundreds of links, uses unrelated sites, or asks for admin access to the CMS.
- Treat the purchase as a draft recommendation for the human to approve and pay for.

## Part 2: Launch Platforms

Launch platforms give a short spike of visits, a permanent profile link, and a chance of coverage in newsletters and blogs that track new products.

### Platform types

- **Product Hunt:** free, strongest for software, apps, and tools. Best for products with a clear one-line value statement and visuals.
- **BetaList and similar early-stage lists:** some charge a fee or have a waitlist. Check current terms before recommending.
- **Niche launch sites:** search `[niche] launch platform`, `[niche] product directory`, `Product Hunt alternatives for [niche]`, `where to launch a [niche] startup`.
- **Communities with show-and-tell threads:** only where self-promotion is explicitly allowed. Follow the thread rules and disclose that you are the maker.
- Platforms change policies and shut down; verify each one is active and what it costs before including it.

### Prepare a complete listing

Incomplete launches get little exposure and may be placed in a low-visibility view. Before launch:

- Name, tagline, short description within the platform limit, topics, links.
- Gallery: 3 to 5 clean images or a short video, with real product shots.
- Maker comment: who you are, why you built it, what is new, and a clear ask (feedback, not upvotes).
- Pricing and free tier stated plainly.
- Website landing page ready for the traffic, with analytics and a visible next step.
- A support contact so the team can reply quickly on launch day.

### Launch rules

- Do not ask for upvotes through mass messages, groups, or paid services. Ask people to try the product and give honest feedback. Vote rings are against platform rules and get listings removed.
- Disclose affiliation in every comment.
- Reply to every comment on launch day.
- If a complete listing is stuck in a low-visibility view, contact platform support politely and ask whether it can be reviewed.

### Relaunch cadence

- Launch once at first release. Relaunch only for meaningful updates, such as a major feature, new pricing model, or new product line.
- Space relaunches at least 3 to 6 months apart, and check each platform's stated policy.
- Each launch needs fresh material: new screenshots, a changelog summary, and a new angle.
- Log every launch in the tracker with the results (visits, signups, coverage, new referring domains).

### Measure

Record launch date, platform, listing URL, referral traffic, signups, new referring domains within 30 days, and any press or newsletter pickups. Use the results to decide which platforms deserve a relaunch.
